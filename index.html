Pankaj Kumar

I am a third-year PhD scholar in Computer Science at the National Institute of Science Education and Research (NISER), Bhubaneswar, and a member of SMLab, supervised by Dr. Subhankar Mishra.

My research focuses on the robustness of modern machine learning systems, with a focus on large language models, graph foundation models and knowledge-graph question answering pipelines. I study how these systems fail under adversarial and distributional shifts and trace each failure to the component responsible. My long-term goal is to use these insights to build robust hybrid architectures that integrate graph neural networks with large language models, combining structured relational reasoning with the flexibility of language.

Reliability of multi-agent LLM systems

When Agents Lie: Verifying Multi-Agent Claims Before Voting

Pankaj Kumar, Subhankar Mishra

2026Under review
media/teaser-agents.png

Multi-agent LLM systems settle answers by voting or debate, so a majority of agents arguing for a wrong answer with invented evidence can win decisively. We propose an input-grounded verification framework in which a coordinator checks each claim against its own dispatch record and excludes agents with rejected claims from the vote, cutting attack success from above 0.97 to 0.025 or below with no external knowledge source and no extra model call. Efficient long-context inference

PAGE: Partition-Aware Gated KV-Cache Eviction

Pankaj Kumar, Subhankar Mishra

arXiv preprint, 2026Under review
media/teaser-page.png

The key–value cache grows with context length and quickly dominates memory during long-context LLM inference. PAGE is a gated, partition-aware policy that decides when cached entries can be safely evicted, preserving generation quality under a fixed memory budget.

Robustness of graph foundation models

Attacking Graph Foundation Models Through Their Shared Representation

Pankaj Kumar, Subhankar Mishra

arXiv preprint, 2026Under review
media/teaser-gfm.png

Every graph foundation model maps its inputs into one shared representation before any task reasoning, which makes that alignment layer an attack surface in its own right. We evaluate representation-space and realizable input-space attacks on six public graph foundation models spanning spectral tokenizers, text embedding spaces and a discrete codebook.

Reliability of knowledge-graph question answering

Query-Side Attacks on GNN-Based KGQA: Tracing Failures from Entity Linking to Answer Generation

Pankaj Kumar, Subhankar Mishra

arXiv preprint, 2026Under review
media/teaser-kgqa.png

GNN-based KGQA pipelines pass a question through entity linking, subgraph retrieval, GNN reasoning and answer generation. Isolating each stage under answer-preserving perturbations on ComplexWebQuestions and WebQSP shows that subgraph construction, not GNN reasoning, drives the end-to-end collapse, separating answer presence from answer reachability.

Robustness of large language models

Robustness in Large Language Models: A Survey of Mitigation Strategies and Evaluation Metrics

Pankaj Kumar, Subhankar Mishra

Transactions on Machine Learning Research, 2025TMLR
media/teaser-survey.png

Reliable deployment of LLMs needs robustness well beyond resistance to adversarial inputs. This survey organises the landscape across out-of-distribution generalization, bias and fairness, contextual understanding and shortcut learning, together with the mitigation strategies and evaluation metrics used for each.
