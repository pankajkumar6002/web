Pankaj Kumar

I am a third-year PhD scholar in Computer Science at the National Institute of Science Education and Research (NISER), Bhubaneswar, and a member of SMLab, supervised by Dr. Subhankar Mishra.

My research focuses on the robustness of modern machine learning systems, with a focus on large language models, graph foundation models and knowledge-graph question answering pipelines. I study how these systems fail under adversarial and distributional shifts and trace each failure to the component responsible. My long-term goal is to use these insights to build robust hybrid architectures that integrate graph neural networks with large language models, combining structured relational reasoning with the flexibility of language.

Research

Efficient long-context inference

Click here for more info

The key–value cache grows with context length and quickly dominates memory during LLM inference. I study when cached entries can be safely evicted, using a partition-aware, gated eviction policy that preserves generation quality under a fixed memory budget.

Preprint · Under review

Robustness of graph foundation models

Click here for more info

A graph foundation model maps every input into one shared representation, the alignment layer, before any task reasoning. This work identifies the alignment layer as a distinct attack surface and studies inference-time attacks on six public graph foundation models spanning spectral tokenizers, text embedding spaces and a discrete codebook.

Related: Attacking Graph Foundation Models Through Their Shared Representation

Preprint · Under review

Reliability of knowledge-graph question answering

Click here for more info

GNN-based KGQA pipelines process a question through four stages: entity linking, subgraph retrieval, GNN reasoning and answer generation. Using a stage-isolation protocol with answer-preserving adversarial perturbations on ComplexWebQuestions and WebQSP, this work shows that subgraph construction, rather than the GNN reasoner, accounts for nearly all of the end-to-end failure.

Related: Query-Side Attacks on GNN-Based KGQA · Code

Survey · TMLR 2025

Robustness of large language models

Click here for more info

Reliable deployment of LLMs requires robustness well beyond resistance to adversarial inputs. My survey in TMLR organises this broader landscape, covering out-of-distribution generalization, bias and fairness, contextual understanding and shortcut learning, together with the mitigation strategies and evaluation metrics used for each.
